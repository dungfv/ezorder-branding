Your invoice is one of the few documents a customer keeps after the parcel is unpacked. Accountants file it, wholesale buyers forward it to their finance team, and some customers print it for warranty claims. Yet most stores still send the generic confirmation Shopify produces by default.

This guide walks through what a good Shopify invoice template contains, how to customize one without touching code, and how to make sure the right invoice reaches the right customer automatically.

## Why customize your Shopify invoice template?

Shopify's built-in order notifications are designed to confirm a purchase, not to serve as an invoice. They are hard to restyle, they don't produce a PDF your customer can file, and they rarely carry the legal details many countries require.

A customized invoice template fixes three problems at once:

- **Brand consistency.** The document looks like your store — your logo, colours and typography — instead of a system printout.
- **Compliance.** You can add the tax number, company registration number, invoice number sequence and payment terms your jurisdiction expects.
- **Fewer support emails.** When the invoice is clear, complete and easy to download, customers stop asking you to resend it or correct it.

## What every invoice should include

Requirements vary by country, but a solid invoice template usually contains:

1. **Seller details** — legal business name, address, email and phone.
2. **Tax and registration numbers** — for example a VAT, GST or sales-tax ID and a company registration number.
3. **A unique invoice number** and the **issue date** (plus a due date for B2B orders on payment terms).
4. **Customer details** — billing and shipping address, and company name and tax ID for business buyers.
5. **Line items** — product name, SKU, variant, quantity, unit price, discounts and tax per line.
6. **Totals** — subtotal, shipping, tax breakdown by rate, grand total, amount paid and balance due.
7. **Payment information** — method, status and any bank details for transfers.

If you sell to businesses, add the **purchase-order number** and **payment terms** your buyers reference. Their accounts-payable team will often reject an invoice without them.

## Invoice template checklist

Before you publish your new template, run through this list:

- Logo is sharp and sized correctly on both A4 and Letter paper
- Legal business name and address match your registration
- Tax and registration numbers are shown where required
- Invoice numbers are unique and sequential
- Discounts, shipping and tax are broken down clearly
- B2B orders show purchase-order number and payment terms
- Labels read naturally in every language you sell in
- Automatic email is enabled for the moment that suits your workflow
